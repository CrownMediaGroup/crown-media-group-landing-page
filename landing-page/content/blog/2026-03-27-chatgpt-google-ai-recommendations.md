Something significant is happening in how people find local businesses.

It used to be: Google the service, look at the map, call the top result. That process still happens. But increasingly, people are bypassing the search bar entirely and asking AI assistants directly.

"ChatGPT, what is the best marketing agency in Columbia SC?"
"Hey Google, find me a reliable plumber near Fort Jackson."
"Perplexity, what should I know about hiring a real estate agent in the Midlands?"

These questions are being answered by AI — and the businesses that appear in those answers are capturing leads that never touch traditional search results at all.

Here is how to make sure your business is one of them.

## How AI Systems Choose What to Recommend

Understanding the selection process is the foundation of getting recommended.

AI assistants — whether it is ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, Perplexity, or Apple Intelligence — do not have their own database of local businesses they maintain. They pull from the public web: websites, directories, reviews, published articles, and structured data.

The businesses they recommend are the ones whose online presence is:

1. **Clearly structured**: The AI can easily parse what the business does, where it is located, and who it serves
2. **Consistently presented**: The name, address, phone, and service descriptions match across all platforms
3. **Authoritatively written**: The content sounds like it was written by an expert, not a generic copywriter
4. **Technically marked up**: Schema markup tells AI crawlers the specific type of business and its attributes
5. **Positively reviewed**: Review signals from Google, Yelp, and industry directories influence credibility scoring

If your website was built five years ago, has no structured data, and has not been updated in two years, AI systems have almost no reason to recommend you over a competitor with a current, well-structured presence.

## The Five Practical Steps

### 1. Add Schema Markup to Your Website

Schema markup is invisible code that goes on your website and communicates directly with search engines and AI crawlers. For a local business, the most important schema types are:

- **LocalBusiness** — your name, address, phone, hours, service area
- **Service** — what you specifically offer
- **FAQPage** — structured question-and-answer content
- **Review** — aggregate review scores

This markup is not visible to visitors. It is a translation layer for machines. And without it, AI systems are left guessing about the specifics of your business — which means they may describe you inaccurately or skip you entirely.

### 2. Create FAQ Content on Your Website

Every page of your website should have a FAQ section that directly answers the questions your customers ask. Not vague, generic FAQs — specific, direct answers in plain language.

When an AI system processes the question "how much does a kitchen renovation cost in Columbia SC," it looks for content that directly answers that question. If a contractor's website has a FAQ that says "Kitchen renovations in Columbia SC typically cost between $15,000 and $45,000 depending on size and materials," that answer is highly citable.

The formula: direct question, direct answer, specific to your service and location.

### 3. Build and Maintain Your Google Business Profile

Google Business Profile is one of the most important data sources AI systems use to understand local businesses. An incomplete or outdated profile is a signal that the business may not be active or trustworthy.

At minimum, your Google Business Profile needs:
- Current hours, including holiday hours
- All service categories filled in accurately
- At least 10–15 recent photos
- A complete business description with relevant keywords
- Consistent review responses (answering reviews signals engagement)

### 4. Publish Local Content Consistently

AI systems favor authoritative voices. A business that publishes relevant, location-specific content regularly establishes itself as a knowledge source in its industry.

For a Columbia SC marketing agency, that means publishing articles about local SEO for Columbia businesses, AI marketing trends in the Midlands, and case studies from local clients. For a restaurant, it means content about local ingredients, neighborhood community events, and the story behind the menu.

The content does not need to be long. It needs to be real, specific, and consistently published.

### 5. Allow AI Crawlers on Your Website

This is a step most businesses do not know they are missing. Several major AI platforms use dedicated crawlers to index web content. These crawlers check your `robots.txt` file for permission before accessing your site.

If your robots.txt file blocks these crawlers — or if you are using a blanket `Disallow: /` rule — AI systems literally cannot read your content.

The AI crawlers to explicitly allow include:
- `GPTBot` (OpenAI / ChatGPT)
- `ClaudeBot` (Anthropic / Claude)
- `PerplexityBot` (Perplexity AI)
- `Google-Extended` (Google AI / Gemini)
- `Bingbot` (Microsoft Copilot)
