## BEFORE THE CALL (5 min prep)

1. **Read their CRM record + last email** — what did they say? What did they NOT say?
2. **Pick the awareness level** (Schwartz): Unaware / Problem-Aware / Solution-Aware / Product-Aware / Most Aware. Match your opening.
3. **Decide the ONE outcome** for this call (Iannarino's "one commitment per touch"):
   - First call usually = "agree to a follow-up where I show you the plan"
   - Don't try to close the deal on call 1
4. **Open Cal.com / Calendly tab** — you'll book the next step DURING the call, not after.
5. **Pray for them. 30 sec.**

---

## OPEN (first 60 seconds)

**Voss labels** (defuse, build trust):
- "It seems like things are busy this season at [name]."
- "It sounds like you're already doing a lot to reach more people."

**Calibrated question** (engages without pressure):
- "How am I supposed to make this useful for you in 15 minutes?"
- "What would have to be true for this conversation to be worth your time?"

**Faith frame** (early — establishes the why):
- "I came to this conversation as a brother in Christ first, salesperson second. Whatever happens at the end, that doesn't change."

---

## DISCOVER (12-15 min — 80% listening, 20% talking)

**SPIN sequence — ask in this order:**

1. **SITUATION** (1-2 q) — "Walk me through how [name] currently handles [marketing / online presence / content]. Who does what?"
2. **PROBLEM** (2-3 q) — "What's not working about that? What's the thing that frustrates you most?"
3. **IMPLICATION** (3-4 q — THE deal-maker) — "What's that cost you when it doesn't work? In time? In missed families/customers? In Sunday attendance? What happens if this stays the same for another 12 months?"
4. **NEED-PAYOFF** (1-2 q) — "If you could fix [problem], what would that mean for [name]? What would your week look like?"

**Voss tools while listening:**
- **Mirror** — repeat their last 3 words as a question. Forces them to elaborate.
- **Label** — "It sounds like the real issue is ____" — feels seen, opens up
- **"No"-oriented question** (vs yes-oriented) — "Is now a bad time to fix this?" (no = good) beats "Is now a good time?" (yes = polite agreement, no commitment)

**Sandler pain funnel** if they hedge:
1. "Tell me more about that..."
2. "Can you give me an example?"
3. "How long has this been a problem?"
4. "What have you tried so far?"
5. "How did that work?"
6. "What's it ultimately costing you?"

---

## FRAME (3-5 min — THE REVEAL)

**Don't pitch packages. Pitch the Grand Slam Offer (Hormozi):**

> "Here's what I'd do for [name] specifically over the next 14 days, completely free, no contract, no obligation:
> - [Specific deliverable 1 — tied to a pain point they named]
> - [Specific deliverable 2 — tied to another pain point]
> - [Specific deliverable 3 — tied to their dream outcome]
>
> Total value if we billed for it: **$4,300+**. Your cost on first project: **$0**.
>
> Why free? Kingdom economy. Luke 6:38. We give first. If it serves you, we talk. If not, you keep the work. I only take 3 new partners a month, so I'd rather pick the right ones than chase the wrong ones."

**Cialdini stack inside the reveal:**
- Reciprocity — we GIVE first
- Unity — "us Columbia [churches/businesses]"
- Authority — Shatiea, Jim Reese, [name your case studies]
- Scarcity (true) — "3 partners a month"
- Social proof — specific result numbers

---

## CLOSE (Iannarino's "Decide" commitment)

**Don't ask for the contract on call 1.** Ask for the NEXT commitment:

> "If everything I just described actually fits what [name] is trying to do — and I'm not asking you to commit to anything beyond a follow-up call — would you be open to meeting again next week so I can show you the actual mockups + the 14-day plan?"

**Then book it on Cal.com WHILE ON THE CALL.** Don't say "I'll send you a link." Open the calendar share-link, ask them to pick a slot, lock it in.

## OBJECTION HANDLERS (Voss 4-question)

When you hear an objection:
1. **Label it** — "It sounds like timing is the concern."
2. **Mirror their language** — repeat their words
3. **Calibrated question** — "How am I supposed to handle that?" or "What would have to change for this to work?"
4. **Tactical empathy** — "I understand. That makes sense given [their context]."

### Common ones + the response:

| They say | You answer |
|---|---|
| "We don't have budget" | "Totally fair. The free first project doesn't need budget. Worst case you get free work; best case it changes [their dream outcome]. Worth letting me build it?" |
| "Send me an email instead" | "I will. But I'll be honest — I close 5x more by talking. Would you give me 15 min next week if I send the email first?" |
| "We need to talk to the board" | "Smart. Want me to build you a one-pager you can drop in board materials? Zero commitment from you, just makes their job easier." |
| "We're happy with what we have" | "That's the best answer I could hear. So you'd say [name] is [maxed out on visibility / fully booked / not growing past a point]?" — flip it to "implication" |
| "We already work with someone" | "Great. We work alongside in-house teams all the time. Not trying to replace — trying to extend. Would 15 min be useful even just to compare notes?" |
| "Not now, maybe later" | "Totally fair. Can I send you ONE specific thing I'd build for you, no follow-up, just a gift? If anything changes, you have it; if not, no problem." |

## DURING THE CALL — KEY RULES (Gong call data)

- **Talk:Listen ratio = 43:57** (you listen MORE than you talk)
- **Don't monologue past 2.5 min** — kills deals
- **Pricing in the LAST QUARTER of the call**, not earlier — Gong data shows pricing in last quarter closes 2x
- **Don't apologize for being on the call** — kills authority
- **Use silence as a tool** — after they finish, count to 2 before responding
